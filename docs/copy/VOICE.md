# How SPARC Labs sounds

*22 Nov 2026. Written by the copy agent for Kyle. Claims are held to climate-home-inspection `docs/validation/VALIDATION-STATUS.md` (§3, the words; §4, the lines that overstate). The page-by-page copy is in `SITE-COPY.md` next to this file.*

## Who is talking

A friend who did a PhD in something with "fluid" in the title, now works in climate tech, still says "lowkey" and thinks that makes them Gen Z. They are genuinely excited about the models. They are also the first to tell you what the models can't do, because they've read the error bars and they respect you.

The formula, every time: **brag about the method, be humble about the answer.**

- The method is real and worth bragging about: a 1 m flood model that runs on your own graphics card, street air temperature hour by hour, satellite radar of the ground, storm gusts carried in from real anemometers. We built it, we tested it in public, we show the receipts.
- The answer for one house is a screening-level first look. We say so first, not in the footer.

## Five rules

1. **Limit first, then the flex.** "Nothing has been measured at your house. Here's what the model shows in a hundred-year storm." Saying the limit first is what makes the flex believable.
2. **Every number has a home.** A figure on the site comes with where it was measured and what it was measured against. No orphan percentages. If we can't say where a number came from, it doesn't go up.
3. **Name the quantity.** Surface temperature (satellite), air temperature (modelled) and PET (outdoor comfort, modelled) are three different things. Never "temperature" alone, never "feels like".
4. **Slang is seasoning.** At most one bit per screen, never in the same sentence as a number or a limit. "The receipts", "lowkey", "no notes" are fine. Nothing that dates in six months, nothing that needs a dictionary, no emoji in body copy.
5. **We are the model, not the oracle.** The model "shows", "puts water at", "carries the gust in from". It never "predicts", "knows" or "measures".

## Word swaps

| We don't say | Because | We say |
|---|---|---|
| predicts, forecast, will flood | No model here has the real-world record for that | "in the model", "in every storm we modelled, water reached the outside walls" |
| accurate, precise, high-accuracy | Nothing is measured at a house | the engine's own error, and where it was measured |
| validated, proven, verified (of a result) | Only "validated at this place", only after a field check | "exploratory", "screening-checked", "checked against …" |
| risk score, risk rating, grade | We don't give one. No colour for the house either | "what the model shows in these storms"; FEMA's index named as FEMA's |
| safe, dry, protected, calm, stable | No model can promise that | "did not reach the outside walls in any storm we modelled" |
| sinking, rising (ground) | Radar reads along one slanted line of sight | "moving toward / away from the satellite, compared with the ground around it" |
| likely, chance of, probability | No calibrated probability exists | "in the storms we modelled" |
| feels like, heat index, real feel | Mixes quantities | "PET, an outdoor comfort index, modelled" |
| hot days, savings, payback, ROI, expected loss | Not allowed in reports (directive §4) | nothing; we just don't |
| "street-level wind", "where buildings funnel wind" | The street wind model fails its building-array tests | "the street wind model is still in the wind tunnel" |

## Persuasion we use, and what we don't

The branding thread owns the playbook. From the copy side, the honest version of each trick:

- **Curiosity, yes.** "Your flood zone is one letter. Your street has a whole story." True, and it makes people click.
- **Specificity, yes.** "88 of 94" beats "industry-leading". Specific numbers read as honest because they are.
- **The pratfall effect, yes.** Admitting a miss (Louisiana, thunderstorm gusts) makes the passes believable. We lead with it on purpose.
- **Free first, yes.** The public record is free with no account. Reciprocity is real and it's also just the product.
- **No fake scarcity, no countdowns, no "3 people are viewing this block".**
- **No fake social proof.** No testimonials, logos or customer counts until they exist. The About page says "people: one" and that's a flex, honestly.
- **No fear.** We never use a flood photo, a disaster headline or "don't get caught out" to sell a report.

## The test before anything ships

Read the line out loud and ask: *if the buyer acted on this sentence and the model were wrong by its own stated error, would they feel lied to?* If yes, rewrite.
