## Post captions

Post them in this order, about two a week. Each image matches the post number.

**01 · One letter** (block art)
> Your flood zone is one letter. Your street has a whole story.
>
> FEMA's map gives a house a zone. It doesn't say where water went in the last big storm, how much of the block is pavement, or whether the ground is higher or lower than the street.
>
> All of that is public. It's just scattered. Type any US address at sparclabs.co and read the whole record, free, with the source on every line.

**02 · Who checks the block?** (logo)
> Your inspector checks the house. Who checks the block?
>
> That's the whole idea behind SPARC Labs. Water, heat, shifting ground and wind don't stop at the property line, so a check that stops at the walls misses half the story.

**03 · Physics, not a crystal ball**
> People ask how a flood model works. Here's the honest version.
>
> Water goes downhill, soaks in where the soil lets it, and slows where the ground is rough. We know those equations. Give a computer the shape of the ground to about a meter, how rough the streets and yards are, what's paved and how fast each soil drains, then give it a storm.
>
> It shows what happened in the storms we ran. It doesn't promise what will happen. Every result says how much evidence stands behind it, and today that mostly says "exploratory". We print that word big.

**04 · Things we will never tell you**
> Things we will never tell you:
> × That a house is safe. No model can promise that, ours included.
> × A dollar figure for a disaster.
> × A risk score we made up. FEMA's ratings are shown as FEMA's.
> × That a result is proven before it's measured.
>
> We'd rather you trust us for what we won't say.

**05 · Try a French drain before you dig one** (the backyard story; this is a good one to tell in your own words)
> Growing up, our neighbor raised their yard to level it out. All of their water ended up in one spot: our backyard. My dad put in a French drain and it fixed it.
>
> Nobody could have shown us that coming. Now the house report can: add a French drain, a rain garden or a rain barrel, and see how water moves around the house in our flood model.

**06 · What does your block remember?**
> Five questions the public record can answer about any US block:
> · Is it in a FEMA flood zone?
> · Have people nearby made flood insurance claims?
> · Is the ground higher or lower than the streets around it?
> · How much is trees, and how much is pavement?
> · Which hurricanes passed close?
>
> Free at sparclabs.co. No account.

## Setting up each account

**LinkedIn company page** (do this first: it's where agents, inspectors and investors look)
1. From your personal profile: For Business → Create a Company Page → Company.
2. Name SPARC Labs; URL linkedin.com/company/sparc-labs (or the closest free one); website sparclabs.co; industry "Environmental Services" or "Software Development"; size 0–1.
3. Upload `profile-logo.png` and `company-banner.png`; paste the tagline and About.
4. Add yourself as the founder on your profile's Experience, and put `personal-banner.png` on your profile.
5. Post 01, then invite your connections to follow the page.

**Instagram**
1. Make a new account (not your personal one) with a business email; handle @sparclabs or the closest free one.
2. Settings → Account type → switch to a Professional account → Business. That unlocks the link in bio, insights and link stickers on stories.
3. Upload `profile-logo.png`, paste the bio, put sparclabs.co as the link.
4. Post 01 to 03 on day one so the grid isn't empty. Then pin 02 (who checks the block) to the top.
5. Save the three stories as a Highlight called "Start here".

**Keeping it light on your time:** batch a month of posts in one sitting and schedule them (Meta Business Suite schedules Instagram for free; LinkedIn has a built-in scheduler). Turn off notifications. Check messages once a day.
