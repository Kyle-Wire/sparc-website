## The idea in one line

Be the friendly person with the bucket of water. Every piece sends people to the **free block record** on sparclabs.co (live today, no price, no account), and the people you meet in person become the invite list for the house report's soft launch.

That matters because sale is invitation-only for now (the launch plan's phase 3, 5 to 10 buyers). A stranger at a market who looked up their own block, then asked you a question, is the best invite there is. So the goal of the street work is **conversations and lookups**, not sales. Print never shows a price.

## Where to go, best first

Ranked by how close the audience is to buying a house and by cost per real conversation.

| # | Channel | Why | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **First-time homebuyer classes** run by housing counseling agencies | The exact buyer, at the exact moment, sitting down | Free | Offer a 10-minute "what your block remembers" segment. Teach, don't sell. |
| 2 | **Civic association and community meetings** in places that have flooded (e.g. Manayunk's Main Street, Eastwick) | You already do community planning; you know this room | Free | Bring the bucket. Show their own block on a laptop. |
| 3 | **Real estate agents and home inspectors** | They meet buyers every week; one agent is many buyers | Cards | Give them free lookups for clients. Never leave cards at an open house without the agent's yes. |
| 4 | **Hardware stores, garden centers, co-ops, libraries, cafes, laundromats** (bulletin boards) | Hardware and garden shoppers are the French drain and rain barrel crowd | Flyers | Ask the manager first. Flyer has tear-off QR tabs. |
| 5 | **Farmers markets, street fairs, block parties** | Volume, relaxed people, weekends | $35 to $50 a day at Food Trust markets | Food Trust limits non-food stalls; ask for a community or info table (below). Street fairs and block parties are easier. |
| 6 | **Parks** | Free, flexible | Free | A chair, the sign, cards. For anything like an organised table, check with Philadelphia Parks & Recreation first. |
| 7 | **Philadelphia Water Department's Rain Check crowd** | Homeowners already thinking about rain barrels and rain gardens: the what-if hook, exactly | Free | Ask whether you can table near their workshops or leave cards. |
| 8 | **Paid outdoor ads** (bus shelters, digital screens, billboards) | Reach, and it feels big | $1,500 and up a month | Only after channels 1 to 7 tell us what message works. See costs. |

**Where not to put things.** Philadelphia doesn't allow signs or stickers on poles, trees, shelters and other public property. Flyers go on boards where the owner says yes, and stickers get handed out, not stuck up. A fine or a "littering startup" post would cost more than the flyers.

## The sequence

| When | What | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| **Now to February** | Order the starter kit. Indoor rooms: homebuyer classes, civic meetings, libraries, agents and inspectors, hardware store boards. Practise the talk on 10 friendly people. | Winter is quiet for markets but busy for meetings, and agents are planning for spring. |
| **March to June** | Spring buying season. Street fairs, markets as they open, a booth on weekends. Flyers refreshed weekly at the boards that scan best. | Most buyers shop now. |
| **July and August** | Heat angle at markets and pools ("how warm does your street stay at night?"). | People feel it. Keep the heat wording exploratory. |
| **August to October** | Flood angle after storms, at civic meetings. | Hurricane season; people remember the last storm. |
| **After 8 to 12 weeks of data** | One paid test (one month of digital screens or a few junior posters near the best-scanning places). | Spend money only where free placements proved the message. |

## Counting what works (QR tracking)

Every QR opens `sparclabs.co/how-it-works` with tags:

```
?utm_source=flyer&utm_medium=print&utm_campaign=street&utm_content=fl-07
```

- `utm_source` is the piece (flyer, tab, card, sticker-qr, qr-card, booth, shelter).
- `utm_content` is the placement. Make a batch for one spot with `python3 src/build.py --code fl-07 && node src/render.cjs`; the files come out as `print/*-fl-07.pdf`.
- Write each placement in [placements.csv](placements.csv): code, piece, where, date up, date checked, scans, notes.

Two things to settle on the site (the website thread's job, not done here):
1. **Check the analytics records UTM tags.** I couldn't reach sparclabs.co from here to check which analytics it runs. Vercel Web Analytics shows UTM sources if it's on.
2. **Short codes, later.** A `sparclabs.co/go/fl-07` redirect would make the QR codes smaller and let us change where they point after printing. The current codes work without it.

**What counts:** scans per placement per week, then lookups, then people who ask for an invite. **Rules decided now, before the data:** a board with no scans in three weeks gets moved; a channel gets more money only after it beats the others on lookups per dollar for a month.

## The honesty rules for print and talk

The site's claims bar applies on paper too:
- Never "safe", "predict", "accurate", "will flood", "likely", a risk score of ours, a dollar loss or a premium.
- Model results are exploratory until measured. Say it out loud; it's the pratfall that makes people trust the rest.
- **No price in print** until sale opens to everyone.
- **Print the kit only once the product is live.** The what-if card describes the what-if tool as part of the house report, with no "coming soon", so it goes out only after that tool ships.
- Contrast with score sites by method, not by name: "a score is one number you can't check; we show the record, the sources and the limits." Print never names a competitor.

## Ideas worth trying

- **A table-top flood tray.** A clear storage tub, a cardboard or sand block with little houses, a watering can. People pour, water finds the low house. It's the bucket demo as a toy, and kids drag their parents over.
- **"Block record on the spot."** A tablet at the table: type their address, show their own record. Two minutes, and it's personal.
- **Flood walks with a civic group.** Walk the block after rain, point at where water pooled, then show the model. Works as a neighborhood event.
- **The what-if card at hardware stores.** "Try a French drain before you dig one" next to the drainage aisle, with the manager's okay.
- **A sticker for people who looked.** "I read my block's record." Fun, shareable, sends no one to a page they haven't seen.
